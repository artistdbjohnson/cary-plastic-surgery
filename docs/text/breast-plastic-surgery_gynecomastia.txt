Model
Gynecomastia is the overdevelopment or enlargement of male breast tissue. It may affect one or both sides of the chest and can involve excess glandular tissue, localized fat, stretched skin, or a combination of these concerns. Male breast reduction surgery removes and reshapes this tissue to create a flatter, more proportionate chest contour.
Hormonal changes, heredity, weight changes, certain medications, medical conditions, and substances such as anabolic steroids can contribute to chest enlargement. Fat-dominant pseudogynecomastia is not the same as glandular gynecomastia, so a careful evaluation is important before choosing surgery. When appropriate, Dr. Hanna may recommend medical testing or evaluation of an underlying cause before developing a surgical plan.
Key Benefits
A flatter, firmer chest contour with improved proportion.
Reduction of excess fat, glandular tissue, and loose skin when needed.
Greater comfort and confidence in fitted clothing, exercise, and daily activities.
What to Expect
Consultation includes a review of your health history, medications and supplements, possible contributing factors, and the balance of fat, glandular tissue, and skin. Surgery is usually an outpatient procedure lasting about one to three hours under general anesthesia or intravenous sedation. Many patients return to desk work and light activities in one to two weeks, while strenuous exercise and heavy upper-body activity are restricted for approximately four to six weeks. Costs typically range from $6,000 to $10,000, depending on whether treatment requires liposuction, gland excision, skin removal, or a combination.

Benefits of Gynecomastia Surgery
Male breast reduction is designed to improve chest proportion by treating the tissue responsible for persistent fullness. Potential benefits include:
A flatter, firmer, and more masculine chest contour.
Removal of glandular tissue that does not respond to diet or exercise.
Reduction of localized chest fat through liposuction when appropriate.
Improved nipple and areola position or stretched skin in more advanced cases.
Greater comfort during exercise and more confidence in fitted shirts or swimwear.

Ideal Candidates
Good candidates are healthy men whose breast development has stabilized and whose condition has not improved with appropriate medical care or lifestyle changes. Candidates should generally:
Have persistent glandular tissue, localized fat, loose skin, or asymmetry that causes concern.
Be at a relatively stable weight and understand that surgery is not a substitute for weight loss.
Be nonsmokers or willing to stop smoking as directed before and after surgery.
Have realistic expectations about scars, symmetry, sensation, and the time required for swelling to resolve.
Have possible medication, hormonal, substance-related, or medical causes evaluated before elective treatment.
Adolescents may sometimes be considered when development is stable and symptoms persist, but continued breast development can create a need for future treatment. A new lump, nipple discharge, significant pain, or one-sided change should receive medical evaluation before cosmetic surgery is considered.
Technique Options
The appropriate technique depends on whether chest fullness is caused primarily by fat, firm glandular tissue, excess skin, or a combination.
Technique
Description
Ideal For
Liposuction
Removes localized fatty tissue through several small incisions using a thin cannula. Liposuction does not remove firm glandular tissue.
Fat-dominant fullness with good skin elasticity.
Glandular Excision
Removes dense glandular tissue through an incision commonly placed along part of the areola border.
Firm tissue beneath the nipple or fullness that cannot be corrected with liposuction alone.
Combined Liposuction & Excision
Uses liposuction to refine fatty areas and direct excision to remove glandular tissue and shape the chest.
Mixed fatty and glandular gynecomastia.
Skin & Areola Adjustment
Removes excess skin and may reduce or reposition the areola when significant enlargement has stretched the tissues.
More advanced gynecomastia with sagging skin or an enlarged, low areola.
Dr. Hanna will explain which tissue needs treatment, where incisions would be placed, and the scar tradeoffs of each option. Do not stop a prescription medication without guidance from the prescribing clinician.
Gynecomastia Surgery, Step-by-Step
Gynecomastia surgery is usually performed as an outpatient procedure under general anesthesia or intravenous sedation and commonly lasts one to three hours. Your procedure may include:
Anesthesia:
Medication is administered for comfort based on the procedure and your health.
Incisions:
Small liposuction access points or discreet incisions near the areola are planned according to the tissue being treated.
Fat and Gland Removal:
Excess fat is reduced with liposuction, firm glandular tissue is excised, or both techniques are combined.
Contour Refinement:
The chest is shaped for symmetry; excess skin or the areola is adjusted when necessary.
Closure and Support:
Incisions are closed, dressings and a compression garment are applied, and temporary drains may be used in some cases.
You will usually return home the same day with detailed aftercare instructions and scheduled follow-up.
Recovery & Aftercare
Soreness, bruising, tightness, and swelling are expected early in recovery. Many patients return to desk work within one to two weeks, while heavy lifting, chest workouts, and strenuous activity are restricted for approximately four to six weeks or until Dr. Hanna clears them.
First several days:
Rest, walk periodically, take medication as directed, and protect the incisions and any drains.
Weeks 1–2:
Continue wearing the prescribed compression garment and gradually resume light daily activities.
Weeks 3–6:
Increase activity only as directed and avoid pressure, heavy lifting, and upper-body exercise until cleared.
Months 3–6:
Residual swelling continues to resolve and the chest contour becomes more defined; scars continue fading over time.
Maintain a stable weight, avoid nicotine, attend follow-up visits, and contact the office for increasing pain, spreading redness, fever, shortness of breath, chest pain, or an unusual heartbeat.
Risks & Complications
Gynecomastia surgery carries risks including bleeding or hematoma, infection, fluid accumulation, poor wound healing, contour irregularities, asymmetry, persistent pain, fat necrosis, and temporary or permanent changes in nipple or chest sensation. Scars are permanent, although they typically fade, and additional surgery may be needed to address a complication or refine the result.
Anesthesia, blood-clot, cardiac, and pulmonary risks also apply. A complete health history, evaluation of possible underlying causes, smoking cessation, and careful adherence to postoperative instructions can reduce risk but cannot eliminate it. Dr. Hanna will review the risks relevant to your health and surgical plan during informed consent.
Individual results vary.

Frequently Asked Questions
Is gynecomastia the same as chest fat?
No. True gynecomastia involves glandular breast tissue, while pseudogynecomastia is primarily excess fat. Many patients have a combination, and the distinction affects whether liposuction, excision, or both are appropriate.
Do I need a medical evaluation before surgery?
Yes. Dr. Hanna will review your health, medications, supplements, substance use, symptoms, and breast development. Testing or another medical evaluation may be recommended when a hormonal condition, medication, or other cause is possible.
How long does gynecomastia surgery take?
Surgery commonly lasts one to three hours, depending on whether treatment involves liposuction, gland excision, skin removal, or combined techniques.
Will male breast reduction leave scars?
Every surgical treatment requires incisions. Liposuction scars are small, while gland or skin excision requires longer incisions that are often placed along natural chest contours or the areola border. Scars fade but do not disappear completely.
When can I return to work and exercise?
Many patients return to desk work within one to two weeks. Heavy lifting, chest workouts, and strenuous exercise are usually restricted for approximately four to six weeks and should resume only after clearance.
Will I need a compression garment or drains?
A compression garment is commonly used to control swelling and support the new contour. Temporary drains may be needed when more extensive tissue or skin is removed.
Are the results permanent?
Results are long-lasting in many cases once swelling resolves, but significant weight gain, certain medications or substances, anabolic steroids, or an untreated underlying condition can cause chest enlargement to return.
Can surgery change nipple sensation?
Temporary numbness or sensitivity changes can occur, and permanent sensation changes are possible. Dr. Hanna will explain how the proposed technique affects this risk.
Does insurance cover gynecomastia surgery?
Most health plans do not cover cosmetic male breast reduction. Some medically documented cases may be considered, but requirements vary by policy and coverage cannot be guaranteed.
